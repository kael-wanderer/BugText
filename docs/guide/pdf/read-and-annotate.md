---
title: Read and annotate PDFs
summary: Read, search, highlight and comment on PDFs, and send your highlights to a note.
---

PDFs open in a tab like any note. You can read, search and highlight them, add comments, and turn your highlights into Markdown with links back to the page.

## When to use it

- You read papers, reports or manuals as PDFs.
- You want to keep quotes from a PDF in your notes.

## How to use it

### Read

The PDF toolbar has **Pages** (thumbnails), previous and next page, a page number box (type a number and press <kbd>Return</kbd>), zoom with **Automatic**, **Fit Width**, **Fit Page** and **Actual Size**, and **Find**. <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>F</kbd> searches the text of the PDF; Find in Folder searches PDFs too.

BugText reopens a PDF at the page where you left it. If another app changes the PDF while it is open, BugText reloads it, or asks first when you have unsaved edits.

### Highlight and comment

1. Select text on the page. A bar appears with four highlight colours, **Comment**, **Copy Quote** and **Delete**.
2. Click a colour to highlight. Click **Comment** to add a note to the highlight.
3. **PDF: Annotations** (<kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>⇧</kbd> <kbd>A</kbd>) opens a panel listing every highlight and comment; filter them, or click one to go to its page.

### Quote into a note

- **Copy Quote** (<kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>⌥</kbd> <kbd>C</kbd>) copies the selected text with a link to its page. Paste it into a Markdown note; clicking the link opens the PDF at that page.
- **Export…** in the annotations panel writes all your quotes and comments to a **New note**, **Insert into current note**, or **Copy to clipboard**, optionally grouped by page with page links.

## What it will not do

- Highlights and comments are stored by BugText, not written into the PDF file, so the file stays exactly as it was. They follow the PDF if you move it inside the folder.
- Scanned PDFs have no text to select until you recognise it. See [Edit a PDF](edit-pdf.md).
